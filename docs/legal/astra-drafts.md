# Part 1. The map

**Recommended launch structure.** The host sells the call or membership. Connect sells the marketplace service and acts as the host’s limited agent for receiving payment. A contribution or tip is a voluntary payment to the named host. Connect is the business that actually charges the card on its Stripe account. It answers payment complaints, administers refunds, and bears its own obligations to customers, Stripe and the card networks. A customer who pays Connect never has to pay the host a second time.

This arrangement puts responsibility for delivering the conversation with the person who sets its price, accepts the engagement and attends it. It also gives the customer one identifiable business to contact about the charge. It depends on disclosing the actual seller before payment, giving Connect genuine collection authority, and securing a payment arrangement that permits it in each launch market. Calling Connect an agent in the Terms alone achieves none of those things.

## 3. Decisions to make before using these documents

1. **Form and fund the contracting business.** Transfer or license founder-owned code, domain, marks and other assets in writing. Review contributor, open-source and AI-assisted provenance. Clear the brand. Put Stripe, video, hosting, email and social developer accounts in the entity’s name. Keep separate finances, records and capital. Obtain insurance quotations for cyber, technology errors, media liability and general liability. The Terms cannot confer entity protection for an unformed business or erase a founder’s own misconduct.
2. **Confirm the seller structure in writing.** My choice is a disclosed host seller, mandatory trader onboarding and a limited collection appointment. Obtain Stripe approval for the actual one-to-one video, projects, memberships, tips, US platform and foreign recipient accounts. Align receipts, bank descriptors, tax invoices and disputes with that answer. Analyse US state agent-of-payee exceptions, UK/EU payment-services and commercial-agent rules and cross-border transfers. A contractual appointment or use of Stripe does not itself secure an exemption. If the rail requires Connect to be principal for a country, change the seller, tax and refund promises together before enabling it.
3. **Choose initial territories.** The host list is the EU 27 plus Canada, Liechtenstein, Norway, Switzerland, the UK and the US. The customer side has no corresponding territory gate. Start with an approved customer-country, host-country and product matrix. Include sanctions, tax, state privacy and biometric law, age assurance, language, payments, local trader rules and cross-border classification. Terms for the US, UK and EU do not clear the other host countries or every customer jurisdiction.
4. **Choose who bears a nonperformance or fraud loss.** My recommendation is for Connect to deliver valid customer refunds promptly, then recover a documented host-caused loss under the Host Agreement. Fund a reserve. Do not make the customer wait for host cooperation, and do not impose automatic personal-bank debits or charge hosts for Connect’s discretionary goodwill.
5. **Choose the tip model.** My recommendation is to launch with Connect absorbing the fee and with no collection deduction from protected tips. Otherwise disable tips wherever card type, country, network rules, all-in pricing or worker-tip rules are unresolved. The customer-paid example in these documents applies only where enabled lawfully. Describing a surcharge as a “payment fee” does not resolve its legality.
6. **Choose contribution and tip limits.** The project ceiling and the tip-total ceiling are $100,000. I recommend much lower initial transaction and rolling limits based on verified exposure, graduated approval for higher amounts, and explicit controls for related-account transfers. A 30-day payout delay is no guarantee against later reversal. Do not advertise tax deductibility, rewards or ring-fenced project funds.
7. **Choose where membership time may expire.** Sell a defined monthly service from one host, nontransferable and with real available capacity. Establish where expiry, cash redemption or unclaimed-property laws require different outcomes. Disable expiry or the product there until supported. Both host and Connect currently earn from ordinary expired value. Do not rely on renaming stored value. Adopt a remedy where promised capacity was insufficient.
8. **Adopt the withdrawal and cancellation economics.** I recommend the express early-start process, proportionate payment for actual lawful partial performance, a post-period booking option, and the express 14-day contractual contribution/tip reversal in the draft. The latter is an additional commercial promise, not a conclusion that every gift attracts a statutory cooling-off right. Reconcile it with seven-day collections. Cap late-cancellation recovery at what is lawful and supportable, credit replacement bookings, and retain the stricter rule where local law requires it.
9. **Adopt collection and dormancy controls.** Keep manual Collect for ordinary use. Choose a finite contact, escalation, alternative settlement and statutory remittance process by host jurisdiction and Stripe restrictions. Protect residual balances of $0.25 or less and closed or incapacitated accounts. Do not treat them as revenue. Decide who funds any unavoidable final-transfer cost.
10. **Fund redress.** I selected AAA by name, a 30-day opt-out, consumer-home hearings, a 25-claim coordination trigger and a 120-day individual court exit. Obtain the applicable AAA review or registration, confirm the rules and fees, fund them and archive the rules supplied to users. I chose a greater-of-$100-or-12-months cap, broader consumer exceptions and no UK/EU consumer cap. A deliberately unusable arbitration process or illusory remedy weakens the entire protection.
11. **Decide tax, classification and host onboarding country by country.** Establish who invoices what, VAT place of supply, marketplace facilitator or deemed-supplier liability, US tax forms and reporting, EU DAC7 and UK platform reporting, applicable deductions and withholding. Collect only the necessary W-9, W-8 or local equivalents through a suitable secure process. Examine actual control, dependence, prices and working practices under US state tests, UK worker rules and EU platform-work rules. Change operations if necessary. Obtain seller identity and right-to-use proof for managers.
12. **Choose a biometric and age model that works in practice.** Separate Stripe Identity from Stripe payout KYC. Document the operator/provider roles and lawful grounds, verify actual image and template retention, prohibit secondary uses unless separately justified, and commission a genuine accessible non-biometric human verification path. Establish human review of underage results, a child-data deletion and safeguarding process, and measures for customer access in countries that require more than an age declaration.
13. **Choose controllers, processors, retention periods and transfer mechanisms.** I treated hosts as controllers for the content of their notes and Connect as their processor for directed storage, with Connect an independent controller for its marketplace and safety records. Confirm those roles against practice. Contract with processors, verify independent-controller notices, transfer mechanisms and subprocessors, adopt a retention schedule by category and jurisdiction, and implement legal holds. Appoint representatives and a data protection officer if required. Do not claim certification or signed safeguards that are absent.
14. **Choose marketing, personalisation and storage settings.** I recommend explicit host-specific opt-in for promotional invitations as well as alerts, no advertising tracking, a usable objection or off switch for personalised suggestions, and direct search. Assess whether any optional measurement requires device consent. Separate necessary receipts and renewal notices from marketing. Include sender identity, postal address and working unsubscribe wherever required.
15. **Choose the safety service level.** Staff public rights, intimate-image and child-safety reporting, evidence preservation, lawful reporting, reasoned decisions and appeals. Verify US reporting and preservation duties, UK online-safety and child-access assessments, EU DSA service classification, size exemptions, transparency reports, trader traceability and authority contacts. Do not promise around-the-clock general monitoring. A short statutory removal deadline still requires operational coverage.
16. **Choose the launch languages and accessibility standard.** Verify the contract, cancellation and order-button language required in each enabled market. Provide accessible web forms, identity alternatives, receipts and a human complaints channel. Assess applicable accessibility duties, including the European Accessibility Act, without presuming an exemption. Keep mobile-store payments, push notifications and app-store disclosures for a separate launch review.
